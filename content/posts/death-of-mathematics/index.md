> — *Le Monde,* October 12th, 2026

The very first thing we must do is stifle our laughter, as difficult as that might be. All jeers, sneers, and snide remarks must be put aside while our friends in mathematics mourn.

Of all the annoying types of nerds in the world, mathematicians are among the kindest and least pretentious. When the death of physics arrives, we might let our sarcastic remarks fly freely. After all, we've lived under the tyranny of the "Century of Physics," constantly reminded of its outsized greatness, and surely we won't be able to resist a little schadenfreude.

But the mathematicians don't deserve this.

The good news, my friends, is that we are not witnessing the death of mathematics. We are witnessing the ego death of a few ivory-tower individuals, and they are bleating loudly as they bleed out. Their cries have echoed across LinkedIn, Twitter, and private blogs, and have now leaked into mainstream normie spaces like Instagram and Facebook.

The grief is real. There's a video of a Fields Medalist openly weeping circulating social media. Mathematicians have described themselves as mourning, outraged, yet painfully awed by the technology itself.

I find it an odd time for these professors to begin decrying artificial intelligence.

Early on, AI was being linked to the untimely deaths of mentally ill individuals, many of them only teenagers, and the outcry was not heard. Generative algorithms were used *almost immediately* to create pornographic imitations of non consenting women, used to harass and embarrass them. Yet my LinkedIn feed was strangely silent during those times, these past three or so years, at least when it came to the dangers of AI.

But lo! The algorithms have moved beyond sexual blackmail, and now they're writing proofs. **NOW** it's time to be concerned. **NOW** is the time to discuss "the impact of artificial intelligence on our society."

I said I wouldn't engage in snide remarks. My intent here is to build solidarity, to help my mathematician friends see the real problem at play. Proof-writing algorithms are not your enemy. Your enemy is the system of power that allows a handful of people to have an outsized say in what happens to the rest of us.

Consider what has just happened. One institution with access to vast, privately controlled computational and human resources, decided to release 722 AI-generated mathematical manuscripts almost overnight. The rest of the mathematical community was left to sit by and watch, trying to understand, verify, and reckon with what had been dropped on their doorstep.

The same powers that be are foisting these technologies onto our children and teenagers, creating new horrors previously unknown. The same institutions can reshape our access to financial services, culture, and art. They can deploy a software update overnight that radically alters the fabric of our society and **YOU** don't get any say in the matter.

That is the real issue at the heart of the recent controversy in mathematics. It is the same issue at the heart of so many other ills of our time.

So, in this period of mourning, I want to reach out to my mathematician friends and hope they can see the light. Things will never go back to how they were. It cannot be. Together, we can challenge the systems that make these mathpocalypses possible, the mass layoffs, the instruments of addiction, and society-reshaping events. We can demand a say in how our society changes.

And as for the death of mathematics itself, I have even better news: mathematics is far from dead. We are about to enter a golden age of science, technology, engineering and mathematics. Computers surpassed the world's best chess players decades ago, yet more people are playing than ever. Generative AI art has been around for years, and yet my paintbrushes still see the daylight regularly. Algorithms that produce perfectly tuned pop songs have only increased my lust for a live performance. Nothing ever truly dies, it only changes.

Nothing ever truly dies. It only changes.

I'm reminded of the early days of generative AI "art," when humans were posting brainless, bot-like takes declaring that "art is dead." I saw some advice back then that applies just as well to art as it does to mathematics:

**Nothing is stopping you: pick up a pen and paper and get to work!**
